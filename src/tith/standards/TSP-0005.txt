Abstract
────────

  This document defines durable outbound Jobs owned by a TITH mailer.
  It specifies stable identity, immutable contents, route commitment,
  worker exclusion, delivery outcomes, source cleanup, control and
  status operations, completion events, and recovery.

1. Introduction
───────────────

  A Job is the mailer's durable unit of local outbound work.  It owns
  one immutable TTS‑0005 NetMail Message, zero or more private
  Attachment copies inside that Message, one committed delivery copy,
  and the local metadata needed to deliver and report it.

  The Job model does not depend on an on‐disk spool layout or IPC
  binding.  TSP‑0006 defines the standard operation which creates
  Jobs.  This document defines their lifecycle and the TSP‑0004 IPC
  operations which inspect or explicitly alter it.

  A Job is not a Bundle.  Compatible delivery copies may be aggregated
  into an outgoing Bundle or drawn from several storage containers as
  permitted by TSP‑0002.  Repackaging does not create, reroute, or
  concurrently duplicate a Job.


2. Job Identity and Data
────────────────────────

  Every Job has one stable JobID.  A JobID is the uppercase ASCII
  letter "J" followed by exactly 32 lowercase hexadecimal digits
  representing a randomly generated 128‑bit value.  A collision with
  any retained JobID causes another value to be generated.

  JobID is never regenerated after commitment.  It remains unchanged
  across restart, retry, repackaging, status changes, and Reroute.

  A Job records one nonempty Application name.  It is compared by its
  exact UTF‑8 bytes without normalization or case folding.  The
  Application and binding‐supplied caller identity jointly control
  access to the Job; spelling the Application name grants no authority.

  A Job owns one complete TTS‑0005 NetMail Message.  An Attachment is a
  File child inside that Message.  A Job may have no Attachments and
  MUST NOT impose a fixed Attachment count or contents length beyond
  representability, checked allocation arithmetic, available storage,
  and local resource policy.

  Every Attachment has an immutable private copy controlled by the
  mailer and a stable WireFilename equal to its TTS‑0005 Filename.  The
  private copy never depends on a caller pathname or handle after Job
  commitment.  WireFilenames within one Job are distinct by exact
  UTF‑8 bytes and do not change during the Job's life.

  The complete Message and its Attachment Contents form one delivery
  unit.  A File inside the Message relies on the enclosing Message
  Signature and is not independently delivered, retried, rejected, or
  reported as successful.

  The Job also records any post‐delivery Source cleanup obligation
  created by its submission.  A cleanup record contains Keep, Delete,
  or Truncate, the Source pathname when applicable, and a race‐resistant
  identity for the exact regular file opened during ingestion.  A
  Source pathname is local metadata and MUST NOT be transmitted to a
  TITH peer or derived into Subject.


3. Routing Commitment
─────────────────────

  Before a Job is committed, it has one TSP‑0002 NetMail delivery copy
  durably recorded with:

    • Origin as the selected local AKA;
    • the ultimate Destination address and unlisted PublicKey, if any;
    • the exact next‐hop address and unlisted PublicKey, if any;
    • Active or Passive delivery mode;
    • delivery Class;
    • a retry Timestamp, if any; and
    • the resolved disposition and notification for every permanent
      failure kind.

  The permanent kinds are Unroutable, Loop, Relay-Denied, Rejected,
  and Authentication as defined by TSP‑0002.  Their policies are
  resolved and stored when the delivery copy is created.  An explicit
  complete override may assign the same disposition and notification
  to every kind.

  Active work may be claimed by an applicable schedule.  Passive work
  MUST NOT be claimed by a schedule.  An authenticated Poll may claim
  either mode when the Job is committed to the polling peer's exact
  identity, as specified by TSP‑0002.

  Restart, retry, repackaging, endpoint changes, nodelist changes, and
  ordinary configuration reload MUST NOT change committed routing
  data.  Current schedules determine when eligible Active work runs,
  and current endpoints determine how the exact next hop is contacted.
  Only the explicit Reroute operation may replace a committed next hop,
  mode, or resolved failure policies.


4. States and Worker Claims
───────────────────────────

  A Job has exactly one externally visible state:

    Queued
    Active
    Deferred
    Delivered
    Rejected
    Failed
    Cancelled

  Queued is durable and available to an applicable schedule or Poll.
  Active is durably claimed by exactly one worker for one exchange.  A
  Job MUST NOT occur twice in one exchange, be Active in two
  exchanges, or be selected by a Poll while another worker owns it.

  Attempts is zero when the Job is created and increments durably when
  a worker changes Queued or Deferred to Active.  Failure to commit the
  claim leaves the Job in its previous state and does not increment it.

  Deferred means delivery was not confirmed and retry is delayed.  A
  connection failure, incomplete response, transient local failure, or
  TTS‑0005 Rejected reason 4 produces Deferred without changing the
  committed route.  A response Timestamp becomes the delivery retry
  Timestamp when present.

  Delivered means a complete Accepted response was authenticated and
  made durable locally.  It confirms durable storage by the committed
  next hop or recognition there of an already accepted duplicate.  It
  does not assert that the ultimate recipient Application consumed the
  Message.

  Rejected means a complete remote response permanently rejected the
  Message.  TTS‑0005 reasons 1 and 2 produce Rejected.  Failed means a
  permanent local Unroutable, Loop, or other TSP‑0002 failure.  These
  remain distinct from Deferred.

  Cancelled means the Application cancelled the Job before delivery
  was confirmed.  Cancellation never applies Delete or Truncate.  Once
  Cancelled is durable, the service MAY retire its private contents.


5. Delivery Outcomes and Source Cleanup
───────────────────────────────────────

  Delivered and the complete Accepted response MUST be durable before
  the delivery copy or private contents are retired and before Delete
  or Truncate cleanup begins.  A cleanup failure does not change
  Delivered.

  Keep never changes or removes a Source.  Delete removes the verified
  pathname.  Truncate sets the verified regular file length to zero.
  Before either operation, the service MUST verify with race‐resistant
  host facilities that the pathname still identifies the exact object
  recorded during ingestion.  It MUST NOT open, remove, or truncate a
  replacement object.

  Each Attachment has one cleanup status:

    NotRequested
    Pending
    Complete
    NotFound
    Replaced
    Failed

  NotRequested applies to Keep and to a Source moved into mailer
  ownership during submission.  Pending means authorized cleanup has
  not reached a terminal result.  Complete means it succeeded.
  NotFound means the original path no longer exists.  Replaced means
  it identifies another object.  Failed means a permanent local error
  prevents cleanup.  Pending cleanup survives restart and is retried.

  Rejected and Failed apply the recorded TSP‑0002 failure policy.
  Discard may retire immutable contents only after the terminal state
  and failure record are durable.  Dead-Letter retains them for
  administrative handling.  Neither policy applies Source Delete or
  Truncate.

  JobID, Application, terminal state, idempotency association, routing
  record, result, and cleanup statuses remain queryable after private
  contents are retired and until the administrative purge in section
  10.


6. Control Operations
─────────────────────

  Query, Cancel, Retry, and Reroute take JobID and are authorized
  against the Job's Application.  They are atomic with worker claims.
  An unknown or unauthorized JobID is reported as NotFound so another
  Application's Jobs are not disclosed.

  Cancel changes Queued or Deferred to Cancelled.  It is Busy for
  Active, NotPermitted for Delivered, and idempotently Completed for
  Cancelled.  Rejected or Failed may be cancelled while retained
  contents remain.  Cancel MUST NOT interrupt a live TITH exchange.

  Retry changes Deferred to Queued and clears its delivery retry
  Timestamp.  It may change Rejected or Failed to Queued only while
  immutable contents remain.  It preserves the local AKA, next hop,
  mode, Class, WireFilenames, failure policies, cleanup obligations,
  Message, and Attachments.  It is NotPermitted for Delivered or
  Cancelled.

  Reroute is the explicit administrative operation permitted by
  TSP‑0002.  It is allowed for Queued, Deferred, Rejected, or Failed
  while contents remain, Busy for Active, and NotPermitted for
  Delivered or Cancelled.

  Reroute Route applies the current TSP‑0002 NetMail Route methods.
  Reroute Active or Passive instead commits the exact listed address or
  peer reference in the request.  An explicit target requires the same
  authorization and validation as at submission.  Active requires a
  usable endpoint and satisfaction of caller restrictions when
  committed; Passive does not require an endpoint.

  Failure-Policy absent or Route resolves current TSP‑0002 policies.
  An explicit policy applies to every permanent kind and requires
  authorization.  Reroute atomically commits the resulting exact next
  hop, mode, and policies before changing the Job to Queued.  It
  preserves JobID, Application, local AKA, ultimate Destination,
  Class, Message Signature, Attachments, WireFilenames, and cleanup
  obligations.


7. Query and Control IPC Operations
───────────────────────────────────

  These operations use the TSP‑0004 lexical rules and envelopes.  The
  request forms are:

    TITH-IPC 1
    Query <job-id>
    End

    TITH-IPC 1
    Query <job-id> Paths
    End

    TITH-IPC 1
    Cancel|Retry <job-id>
    End

    TITH-IPC 1
    Reroute <job-id>
    Next-Hop Route|Active "<next-hop-identity>"|
        Passive "<next-hop-identity>"
    [Failure-Policy Route|<disposition> Notify <notification>]
    End

  The Next-Hop and optional Failure-Policy directive each occupy one
  physical line.  Disposition is Dead-Letter or Discard; notification
  is None, Sender, Origin-Sysop, or Both.  Paths requests local Source
  pathnames and requires separate authorization.

  A successful Query result is:

    TITH-IPC-Result 1
    Query Completed
    Job <job-id> <job-state>
    Application "<application-name>"
    Origin "<local-identity>"
    Destination "<destination-identity>"
    Next-Hop Active|Passive "<next-hop-identity>"
    Class "<configuration-name>"
    Failure-Policy Unroutable <disposition> Notify <notification>
    Failure-Policy Loop <disposition> Notify <notification>
    Failure-Policy Relay-Denied <disposition> Notify <notification>
    Failure-Policy Rejected <disposition> Notify <notification>
    Failure-Policy Authentication <disposition> Notify <notification>
    Created <timestamp>
    Changed <timestamp>
    Attempts <unsigned-integer>
    Last-Result "<UTF-8-string>"
    [Attachment <one-based-index> "<wire-filename>"
        NotRequested|Pending|Complete|NotFound|Replaced|Failed]...
    [Source-Path <one-based-index> "<local-pathname>"]...
    End

  Each Attachment occupies one physical line.  Attachment lines occur
  in attachment order.  Source-Path lines occur only for path Sources
  in an authorized Paths result and use the corresponding index.
  Created and Changed are TTS‑0003 Timestamps.  Changed is the time at
  which the current state was committed.  Last-Result is empty at Job
  creation and otherwise describes the most recent delivery or control
  result.

  An unsuccessful Query result is:

    TITH-IPC-Result 1
    Query NotFound|TemporaryFailure
    End

  A control result has one of these forms:

    TITH-IPC-Result 1
    Cancel|Retry|Reroute Completed|Busy|NotPermitted|
        TemporaryFailure|PermanentFailure <job-state>
    End

    TITH-IPC-Result 1
    Cancel|Retry|Reroute NotFound
    End

  The operation and result alternatives occupy one physical line.
  Every control result except NotFound includes the current state.

  After an incomplete control result, the Client MUST Query the Job
  before deciding whether another state change is required.  It MUST
  NOT blindly repeat Reroute Route because current configuration may
  resolve differently.  Cancel and Retry may be requested again when
  the queried state still permits their defined transition.


8. Completion Events
─────────────────────

  A service MAY provide completion events or callbacks.  If provided,
  event creation is part of the same durable atomic transaction as
  every externally visible Job state transition.  The new state MUST
  NOT become visible without its event.

  EventID is JobID, Colon (U+003A), and a one‐based decimal sequence
  number without leading zeroes.  Events for one Job are ordered by
  this number.  An event contains EventID, JobID, previous and current
  state, transition Timestamp, and the current Last-Result.

  Event delivery is at least once.  A consumer acknowledges EventID;
  the service retains and retries unacknowledged events.  Consumers use
  EventID to discard duplicates.  Event delivery failure never changes
  Job state or retries a delivered Message.

  All callback and IPC consumers for one Application share one
  acknowledgement namespace and form one consumer group.  An
  acknowledgement by one consumer applies to that EventID for all of
  them.  Delivery alone is not acknowledgement unless trusted callback
  configuration explicitly defines successful callback return that
  way.  A separate fan‐out subscription identity is not defined.

  An untrusted request MUST NOT supply an executable callback or event
  endpoint.  Trusted host configuration associates callbacks or
  subscriptions with Application.

  A service offering IPC event subscriptions accepts:

    TITH-IPC 1
    Events "<application-name>" Now|Wait
    End

  Now atomically selects all unacknowledged events for the Application
  when the operation begins.  Wait does the same if any are available;
  otherwise it waits and selects the complete nonempty set available
  in one atomic snapshot.  Events created later wait for another
  request.  A local interruption returns TSP‑0004 Error with
  TemporaryFailure.

  A successful result is:

    TITH-IPC-Result 1
    Events Completed
    [Event
    Event-ID "<event-id>"
    Job <job-id>
    Previous None|<job-state>
    Current <job-state>
    Changed <timestamp>
    Last-Result "<UTF-8-string>"
    End]...
    End

  Event blocks are ordered first by Changed and then by EventID exact
  bytes.  None is used only for Job creation.  Wait returns at least
  one Event; Now may return zero.

  A consumer acknowledges one event with:

    TITH-IPC 1
    Acknowledge "<application-name>" "<event-id>"
    End

  Its result is:

    TITH-IPC-Result 1
    Acknowledge Completed|NotFound
    End

  Completed is idempotent for an already acknowledged EventID while
  its Job record remains retained.  An unknown or unauthorized EventID
  returns NotFound.


9. Durability, Recovery, and Security
────────────────────────────────────

  Durable means acknowledged state survives a service or system
  failure and an ordinary system restart.  The service MUST complete
  the host's durable storage operation for all affected data and
  metadata before returning a result which acknowledges that state.

  Job creation, worker claim, state transition, Reroute, cleanup
  status, and event creation or acknowledgement are atomic with
  respect to concurrent workers and recovery.  Recovery releases or
  reclaims an abandoned Active claim without allowing concurrent
  transmission.

  When recovery cannot determine whether a complete remote Accepted
  result became durable locally, the Job becomes Deferred with the
  same next hop.  TTS‑0005 duplicate acceptance makes retransmission of
  the identical Message safe.

  Private contents and Job metadata MUST NOT be writable by the
  Application after commitment.  Local pathnames SHOULD be omitted
  from normal logs.  Path access, explicit routing, failure overrides,
  controls, and status detail require authorization against the caller
  identity supplied by the IPC binding.


10. Administrative Retention
────────────────────────────

  An acknowledged event payload MAY be retired while its Job remains,
  but the Service retains enough acknowledgement state for a repeated
  Acknowledge to return Completed.  Unacknowledged events are retained
  until acknowledged or explicitly discarded by an administrator.

  An explicit administrative purge may remove a Job only when it is
  Delivered, Rejected, Failed, or Cancelled and no cleanup status is
  Pending.  The administrator explicitly authorizes retirement of any
  retained Dead-Letter contents and discard of any unacknowledged
  events.  Purge MUST NOT silently occur as ordinary age‐based Job
  processing.

  A completed purge durably removes the Job metadata, its idempotency
  mapping, remaining private contents, cleanup records, and event or
  acknowledgement history as one administrative action.  Afterward,
  Query and Acknowledge return NotFound, Lookup-Submission reports
  NotFound, and the former Idempotency-Key may be reused for a new Job.
  Removing contents alone does not permit key reuse.

  The purge mechanism, retention duration, and administrative
  authorization are host policy and are not local Application IPC
  operations.  A service may retain terminal Jobs indefinitely.


11. Feedback and Discussion
───────────────────────────

  A stable Job separates application submission from spool containers
  and connection attempts.  It also provides one place to define when
  a source may be forgotten, when cleanup is safe, and how an
  uncertain Accepted result is retried without changing its route.

  Job semantics are independent of whether the Application used a
  socket, an atomic request file, a CLI wrapper, or another binding.
